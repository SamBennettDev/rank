Methods and feature reference

The common probability form for strength models is sigmoid(a × (r_home − r_away) + b × venue_effect). Coefficients are fit on pregame examples from earlier seasons. Equal shrinkage applies to all teams. The venue effect used by the classical methods is fit from this season; neural models learn a shared historical venue coefficient. This is a generic game effect, not a team-specific preseason rating.

Massey variants minimize the weighted squared error of margin − (r_home − r_away + H × venue), plus ridge penalties. Colley solves (L + 2I)r = 1 + (wins − losses)/2, then centers at .500. Bradley–Terry minimizes pairwise binary cross entropy plus ridge. Offense/defense fits use metric = intercept + offense_team − defense_opponent + H × venue, weighted by snaps for play statistics. The score fallback preserves coverage when play data is missing.

The evidence blend forms U = 0.5 × 60 × (EPA offense + defense) + 0.5 × 100 × (success offense + defense) + special-team weight × adjusted special EPA. Reliability is c/(c+n) divided by (1+|U−M|/d), with current-season evidence c, design constants n and d, and margin strength M. Final strength is (1−a×reliability)M + a×reliability×U. The factor constants translate per-play scales for the grid; they are not claims of exact football point equivalence.

The rank net is a linear residual plus an evidence-gated tanh hidden layer shared by every team. The gate uses standardized-input clipping consistently with inference. The matchup net forms differences and mean context; its logit is one half of g(difference, context) minus g(negative difference, context), plus venue. Swapping teams and reversing venue therefore gives the negative logit. An equal neutral snapshot gives .500.

Graph inputs concatenate 64 current-season numeric features, means of those features across played opponents, two-hop means of the first 16 features, and opponent standard deviations for eight strength/evidence features. The graph is built only from games before the cutoff. This is a fixed GraphSAGE-style neighborhood aggregation followed by a learned shared neural network, not a claim that the entire graph propagation is end-to-end learned.

Training uses analytic-gradient L-BFGS, binary cross entropy, optional focal weighting, optional Huber loss against clipped margin/12, and ridge. Scaling uses past training examples only. Four-year half-life weighting is global across historical examples, not a carried team rating. Three seeds average logits; a past-outcome temperature calibrates the resulting probabilities. The historical market teacher changes labels, not features: target = (1−alpha) × winner + alpha × de-vig market probability when available.

Named classical algorithms are explicit equal-start variants in this code. LRMC is a margin-based Markov implementation; it is not the original authors’ published fitted model. Elo estimates its generic venue effect from the available current-season snapshot before replaying the earlier games. All implementations are exposed in models.py for review.

Prediction dates and evaluation

Weekly predictions begin with Week 4 and use Monday 12:00 UTC snapshots. Only games with kickoff plus a six-hour completion allowance before the cutoff can supply features. Postseason games are ordered by actual dates, not their misleading source week labels. Historical frozen Week 6 cutoff is the latest completed regular-season Week 6 kickoff plus six hours; 2026 uses the stored October 11 08:30 UTC snapshot.

Winner accuracy gives a .500 probability half a correct pick. Brier score averages squared probability error; log loss averages negative log probability of the observed winner; lower is better. Margin error is reported when a model has an explicit margin forecast. The season-block bootstrap resamples 20,000 sets of entire seasons with a fixed seed, preserving within-season dependence; few available blocks and adaptive design limit interpretation.

The compact play aggregations exclude garbage-time scrimmage plays above score gaps of 38 in period 2, 28 in period 3, and 22 in period 4 or later. Full-play variants are also tested. Special-team EPA is mirrored so one team’s value is the opponent’s negative. Rich metrics include pass/rush EPA, success, yards, explosives, turnover and sack rates, third-down conversion, EPA excluding turnovers, drive EPA, completion rate, and drive field position. No spread columns in the source data are read into model features.
