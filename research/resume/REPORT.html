Rank the season a team earned.

A résumé measures the results a team earned against its schedule. The new standing is actual wins minus the wins a results-based top-25 FBS benchmark would expect against that same schedule.

These percentages describe how often the final standing places a completed game’s winner above its loser, with ties worth half. They are not future-game accuracy, an objective résumé label, or proof of universal superiority. Colley and Bradley–Terry remain competitive.

The scoring rule

Résumé = Σ (game result − benchmark win probability)

A win has result 1; a loss has result 0. If the benchmark would win 20% of the time, winning earns +0.80 and losing costs −0.20. If it would win 95%, winning earns +0.05 and losing costs −0.95. A score of +1 means one more win than the benchmark expects against that schedule. Playing a difficult schedule without winning cannot create positive credit.

Opponent strength is fit from current-season wins, losses and venues using regularized Bradley–Terry. Every team starts equal. When assessing a team, remove every game involving that team, then fit the opponent ratings and choose the 25th-highest FBS strength as its benchmark. This stops a team’s own results from manufacturing opponent quality. Later opponent results revalue earlier games. Road wins receive more credit than equivalent home wins.

The historical calibration selected ridge 0.5 and home advantage 0.3 log odds from 16 settings on 2014–2019 dated future-game probabilities. These shared settings are the same for all teams. The top-25 reference and additive scoring rule are explicit ranking policies; they were not optimized against the current standings, polls, or later-season outcomes. There are no team or division strength priors, score margins, EPA, recruiting, injuries, betting prices, recency bonuses, or preseason rankings in the résumé.

What is better, and what remains uncertain

  • Wins update when opponents establish a stronger or weaker current-season résumé.
  • Losses account for opponent and venue rather than costing an identical point.
  • Flipping a team’s loss into a win, with the same schedule and other results, increases its score by exactly one. Its benchmark probabilities are unchanged because all its games are excluded from the fit.
  • Running up the score cannot improve a résumé; changing only margins leaves every résumé unchanged.
  • The per-game receipts sum exactly to the score. Python and TypeScript independently reproduce the current standings.

Early disconnected schedules and sparse opponent records remain uncertain. Equal priors deliberately avoid assuming an FCS opponent is weak before current-season results provide evidence. An opponent with fewer than three independent modeled results triggers the limited-evidence flag. Supporting opponents outside the modeled field earn zero win credit and cost one for a loss; their quality cannot be estimated reliably from the available field.

The record-probability diagnostic is a Poisson-binomial chance that the benchmark matches or exceeds the observed win total. It assumes conditionally independent games, does not integrate rating uncertainty, and is not a confidence level. It is displayed for interpretation and compared as an alternative ranking, but is not the selected score.

All historical seasons have been inspected in this project. The 2002–2025 comparison is retrospective development, not a pristine holdout. The previous model’s 2014–2019 division calibration also postdates 2002–2013. Historical snapshots use a six-hour completion lag and actual kickoff dates; they do not certify historical publication vintages. There is no objective ground-truth résumé ranking. A new ranking can satisfy clearer earned-results policies without proving it is best at predicting future games.

The separate results-only strength fit predicted winners on 13,594 dated FBS games at 69.24% accuracy (2002–2025 retrospective; log loss 0.5788). This evaluates the opponent-strength component, not the résumé ordering. The original neural forecasts remain a separate predictive study and are not relabeled as résumé quality.
